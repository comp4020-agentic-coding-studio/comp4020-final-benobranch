## Why

[[ANU-036]]'s blur+quantize SVG filter was applied to the whole `#map-svg`
(icons included) with parameters way too strong: the blur washed out the
thin (`stroke-width: 1`) suburb boundary lines entirely, and the 8-level
per-channel color quantization crushed ownership fill colors into the wrong
buckets (user report: "the blue of territories has also been removed").
The actual ask was narrower than what got built — pixelate just the
zone boundaries, and keep them *more* visible than before, not less. The
icons (already pixel art) shouldn't be touched by this filter at all.

## Done when

- Suburb boundary edges read as visibly blocky/stepped (pixelated), not
  smooth anti-aliased curves — but suburb fill colors (including ownership
  tints) are completely unaffected, and the border is clearly visible,
  at least as visible as before ANU-036.
- Icons (data centers, units, bases, factories) are not touched by any
  filter — they stay exactly as rendered before ANU-036.
- `pnpm check` still passes.

## Resolution

Removed ANU-036's `<filter id="map-pixelate-filter">` and `#map-postfx-defs`
from `index.html` entirely, along with `.map-svg`'s `filter: url(...)` in
`map.css` — that filter applied to the whole SVG, icons included, which was
never the ask. Replaced it with `.suburb { shape-rendering: crispEdges; }`:
a rasterization hint that disables anti-aliasing on just the suburb path
fill/stroke, so boundaries render stair-stepped/pixelated without touching
fill or stroke *color* at all — ownership tints (`renderOwnership`'s inline
`style="fill: ..."`) are untouched, and icon layers (separate `<g>`s) were
never affected by a filter scoped to `.suburb` in the first place. Also
bumped the border from a 1px hairline (`#0a4a42`) to a 3px `var(--accent-dim)`
stroke (hover/selected still steps up to the brighter `var(--accent)`) so
boundaries are more visible than before ANU-036, not less, per the "keep them
super visible" ask. `pnpm check` (208 tests) passes unchanged.

## Notes

ANU-036's mistake: used `feGaussianBlur` + `feComponentTransfer` discrete
quantization as a blanket "pixelate the whole map" effect, which is too
blunt an instrument — it degrades exactly the two things (boundary
visibility, ownership color fidelity) that most need to stay legible.
